# 15. L.E.A.N. Leadership and Culture

## 15.1 Role of Leadership in L.E.A.N.

### Leadership is the Foundation

**Why Leadership Matters:**

L.E.A.N. is not a program—it's a way of thinking and acting that must be modeled from the top. Without committed leadership, L.E.A.N. initiatives become:
- Just another "flavor of the month"
- Relegated to a few tools (5S, kanban cards)
- Implemented halfheartedly
- Abandoned when results are slow
- Seen as "the L.E.A.N. department's job"

**With Strong Leadership:**
- L.E.A.N. becomes "the way we work"
- Everyone engaged and empowered
- Problems surface and get solved
- Continuous improvement is daily habit
- Results are sustained and built upon

### What L.E.A.N. Leaders Do Differently

**Traditional Manager:**
```
Office-based
│
├─ Manages by reports and metrics
├─ Focuses on results only
├─ Delegates problems downward
├─ Makes decisions from desk
├─ Tells people what to do
└─ Rewards firefighting
```

**L.E.A.N. Leader:**
```
Gemba-based (where work happens)
│
├─ Goes to see for themselves
├─ Focuses on process AND results
├─ Develops problem-solvers
├─ Makes decisions at gemba with data
├─ Asks questions, coaches thinking
└─ Rewards problem prevention
```

### The L.E.A.N. Leadership Model

**Three Core Responsibilities:**

**1. Go See (Genchi Genbutsu)**
- Observe actual work at actual place
- Understand problems firsthand
- See waste with your own eyes
- Verify data by going to source

**2. Ask Why (Develop People)**
- Ask questions rather than give answers
- Coach problem-solving thinking
- Build capability in others
- Challenge respectfully

**3. Show Respect (Respect for People)**
- Listen to those doing the work
- Act on suggestions
- Create safe environment for speaking up
- Develop people's potential

### Leadership Behaviors Checklist

**Daily Practices:**
- [ ] Spend 60%+ of time at gemba (not in office)
- [ ] Conduct gemba walks with genuine curiosity
- [ ] Ask more questions than give answers
- [ ] Follow up on commitments made
- [ ] Participate in improvement activities
- [ ] Model standard work and discipline
- [ ] Recognize improvement efforts publicly
- [ ] Address problems at root cause level
- [ ] Develop others through coaching
- [ ] Challenge status quo respectfully

**Monthly Practices:**
- [ ] Participate in kaizen events
- [ ] Review improvement project progress
- [ ] Conduct leader standard work audits
- [ ] Teach L.E.A.N. concepts to others
- [ ] Remove barriers for teams
- [ ] Align resources to strategic priorities
- [ ] Celebrate successes and learn from failures

### The Leader's Role in Problem-Solving

**Not This (Firefighting):**
```
Problem occurs
    ↓
Leader fixes it immediately
    ↓
Everyone moves on
    ↓
Problem recurs
    ↓
(Repeat cycle)
```

**But This (Capability Building):**
```
Problem occurs
    ↓
Leader asks: "What do you think we should do?"
    ↓
Coach through problem-solving process
    ↓
Support team in implementing solution
    ↓
Verify effectiveness
    ↓
Standardize and share learning
    ↓
Problem prevented from recurring
```

**Socratic Leadership (Ask, Don't Tell):**

| Instead of Saying: | Ask: |
|-------------------|------|
| "Here's what you should do..." | "What do you think is causing this?" |
| "That won't work because..." | "What might happen if we try that?" |
| "I've seen this before, the answer is..." | "What data do we have? What have you tried?" |
| "Just do it this way." | "What standard are we comparing to?" |
| "You're doing it wrong." | "Walk me through your process. What's working? What's not?" |

### Creating the Right Environment

**Leaders Must Create:**

**Psychological Safety:**
- Okay to surface problems
- Safe to admit mistakes
- Questions encouraged
- No blame culture
- Learning from failures

**Clear Direction:**
- Vision and strategy communicated
- Priorities clear
- Goals aligned top to bottom
- Everyone knows "the why"

**Resources and Support:**
- Time for improvement
- Training provided
- Tools and equipment
- Quick removal of barriers

**Recognition:**
- Celebrate effort, not just results
- Share successes widely
- Acknowledge contributions
- Make improvement visible

## 15.2 Gemba Walks

### What is a Gemba Walk?

**Gemba (現場):** Japanese for "the real place" - where the work actually happens.

**Gemba Walk:**
A structured practice where leaders go to the work area to:
- Observe processes firsthand
- Understand conditions
- Ask questions and learn
- Show respect for people doing the work
- Coach and develop problem-solving
- Verify standard work adherence

**What Gemba Walk is NOT:**
- ✗ An audit or inspection
- ✗ A witch hunt for mistakes
- ✗ An excuse to tell people what to do
- ✗ A quick walk-through with no engagement
- ✗ Looking for someone to blame

**What Gemba Walk IS:**
- ✓ Genuine learning and understanding
- ✓ Coaching opportunity
- ✓ Showing you care
- ✓ Building relationships
- ✓ Developing others
- ✓ Seeing problems firsthand

### The Three Principles of Gemba

**1. Go to Gemba**
- Can't understand from office or reports
- See actual conditions
- Observe actual work
- Real place, real thing, real facts

**2. Check Gemba**
- Look at the standard
- Compare actual to standard
- Identify gaps
- Understand variation

**3. Improve Gemba**
- Based on what you learned
- With the people doing the work
- Make improvements permanent
- Update standards

### Preparing for a Gemba Walk

**Before You Go:**

1. **Check your mindset**
   - Am I coming to learn or to judge?
   - Am I curious or already have answers?
   - Am I rushed or can I engage meaningfully?

2. **Know what you'll observe**
   - Which value stream or process?
   - What standard work exists there?
   - Recent performance (metrics)?
   - Current improvement activities?

3. **Plan your time**
   - 30-60 minutes minimum
   - Can you give full attention?
   - No phone interruptions

4. **Bring notebook**
   - Take notes (not for "gotchas" but for learning)
   - Record observations
   - Note follow-up items

**What to Bring:**
- Notebook and pen
- Curious mindset
- Humble attitude
- Time to engage

**What NOT to Bring:**
- Judgment
- Predetermined solutions
- Distracted multitasking
- Entourage of people

### 15.2.2 What to Look For

**The 4S Framework:**

**1. Safety**
- Are people working safely?
- Proper PPE being worn?
- Housekeeping good (trip hazards)?
- Ergonomics (bending, reaching)?
- Machine guarding in place?
- Near-miss potential?

**2. Standard Work**
- Is standard work posted and visible?
- Are people following the standard?
- Is standard current (reflects actual best method)?
- Tools and materials in standard locations?
- Work sequence as documented?

**3. Status**
- How is performance vs. target?
- Any problems visible on boards?
- WIP levels appropriate?
- Flow or batching?
- Bottlenecks?

**4. Suggestions/Improvements**
- Is team working on improvements?
- Are suggestions posted?
- Implementation happening?
- Results visible?

**Observe the Process, Not Just Results:**

Watch the work:
- Where does operator walk?
- How much searching, reaching?
- Waiting for anything?
- Rework or inspection loops?
- Handoffs smooth or awkward?
- Quality checks in process?

Look for the 8 wastes:
- Defects
- Overproduction
- Waiting
- Non-utilized talent
- Transportation
- Inventory
- Motion
- Extra processing

**Read the Visual Management:**
- Metric boards - on target?
- Problem boards - issues being addressed?
- Andon status - normal or abnormal?
- Kanban cards - system flowing?
- 5S audit scores - maintaining?

### 15.2.3 Asking Questions vs. Giving Answers

**The Power of Questions:**

Questions engage thinking. Answers shut it down.

**Question Framework:**

**Open Questions (Start with these):**
- "Can you walk me through this process?"
- "What are you working on improving?"
- "What problems do you face regularly?"
- "What would make your job easier/safer/better?"
- "How do you know if you're on schedule?"

**Observational Questions:**
- "I notice [observation]. Can you explain why?"
- "I see [X]. What causes that?"
- "Help me understand [this aspect]."

**Coaching Questions (5 Whys):**
- "Why does that happen?"
- "What do you think causes that?"
- "If we fixed [X], what would change?"
- "What have you tried already?"
- "What's stopping us from [improvement]?"

**Respect-Based Questions:**
- "What ideas do you have?"
- "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
- "What support do you need from me?"
- "How can I help?"

**Questions to AVOID:**

- "Why didn't you do [X]?" (Blaming)
- "Don't you know you should..." (Condescending)
- "Who made this mistake?" (Witch hunt)
- Leading questions where you just want your answer confirmed
- Questions when you're not genuinely interested in the answer

### Gemba Walk Example Dialogue

**Poor Approach:**
```
Leader: "Why is there so much inventory here?"
Operator: "Um, that's what we were told to make..."
Leader: "Well, it's too much. You need to reduce it."
Operator: "Okay..." (thinking: Here we go again)
Leader: (walks away, problem not solved, operator feels blamed)
```

**Good Approach:**
```
Leader: "Good morning! Mind if I observe for a few minutes?"
Operator: "Sure, come on over."
Leader: (watches operator work through a cycle)
Leader: "Thanks for letting me see that. I noticed you walked
        to the tool crib twice. What are you getting?"
Operator: "Oh, we keep running out of inserts. I have to go
          get more every few parts."
Leader: "How often does that happen?"
Operator: "Several times a shift. It's frustrating."
Leader: "I bet. How much time does it take each time?"
Operator: "Maybe 5 minutes round trip, so probably 20-30
          minutes per shift total."
Leader: "What do you think we could do about it?"
Operator: "Well, if we had a kanban system or just kept a
          small stock here, I wouldn't have to walk so much."
Leader: "That makes sense. Want to try setting up a two-bin
        system? I can get you bins today, and we can work
        with tool crib to make it happen."
Operator: "Yeah, that would be great!"
Leader: (follows up later, improvement happens, operator engaged)
```

### Gemba Walk Etiquette

**Do's:**
- ✓ Greet people warmly
- ✓ Ask permission to observe
- ✓ Stand where you won't interfere
- ✓ Watch entire work cycles
- ✓ Ask genuine questions
- ✓ Listen more than talk
- ✓ Take notes
- ✓ Thank people for their time
- ✓ Follow up on commitments
- ✓ Come back regularly

**Don'ts:**
- ✗ Interrupt work mid-cycle
- ✗ Stand in safety zones
- ✗ Criticize people
- ✗ Give snap judgments
- ✗ Make promises you can't keep
- ✗ Bring large groups (intimidating)
- ✗ Rush through
- ✗ Only show up when there's a problem
- ✗ Ignore people's ideas

### After the Gemba Walk

**Immediate Actions (Same Day):**

1. **Review notes**
   - What did you learn?
   - Patterns or themes?
   - Questions raised?

2. **Capture follow-ups**
   - Commitments you made
   - Problems identified
   - Ideas to explore
   - Assign owners and due dates

3. **Share observations** (positive first!)
   - Recognize good things seen
   - Share learning with leadership team
   - Connect people with similar challenges

**Example Follow-Up Log:**

| Date | Area | Observation | Action | Owner | Due Date | Status |
|------|------|-------------|--------|-------|----------|--------|
| 1/15 | Mill #2 | Excessive walking for tools | Implement 2-bin system | John | 1/20 | ✓ Done |
| 1/15 | Lathe #1 | Standard work out of date | Update with team | Sarah | 1/25 | In progress |
| 1/15 | Grinding | Great 5S practice | Share photos at mtg | Leader | 1/18 | ✓ Done |

**Follow Up, Follow Up, Follow Up:**

Nothing kills credibility faster than:
- Asking questions but not caring about answers
- Promising help but never following through
- Identifying problems but taking no action
- Making gemba walks feel like empty rituals

**Build Trust Through:**
- Doing what you said you'd do
- Coming back to see progress
- Removing barriers you promised to remove
- Recognizing improvements made
- Being consistent and regular in walks

### Gemba Walk Frequency

**Daily:** Supervisors/Team Leaders
- 30+ minutes on gemba per shift
- Participate in tier meetings at gemba
- Coach and support in real-time

**Weekly:** Department Managers
- 1-2 hours minimum
- Cover all areas in rotation
- Focus on metrics, improvements, barriers

**Monthly:** Plant/General Manager
- Visit all value streams
- Focus on strategic initiatives
- Demonstrate commitment
- Build relationships

**Quarterly:** Senior Leadership
- Understand flow across organization
- See improvements in action
- Reinforce priorities
- Show value placed on gemba

## 15.3 Respect for People

### The Second Pillar of L.E.A.N.

Most people know "Continuous Improvement" is a L.E.A.N. pillar.

Fewer realize "Respect for People" is equally fundamental.

**Toyota's Two Pillars:**
```
        ┌─────────────────────┐
        │    L.E.A.N. / TPS   │
        └──────────┬──────────┘
                   │
         ┌─────────┴─────────┐
         │                   │
    Continuous          Respect for
    Improvement            People
         │                   │
    (Just-in-time,     (Development,
     Jidoka, Flow,      Engagement,
     Kaizen, etc.)      Empowerment)
```

**Without Respect for People:**
- Tools implemented top-down
- Resistance and resentment
- Superficial compliance
- Unsustainable results
- Burnout and turnover

**With Respect for People:**
- People engaged in improvement
- Ideas flow from frontline
- Ownership and pride
- Sustained gains
- Growing capability

### What "Respect" Means in L.E.A.N.

**Not Just "Being Nice":**

Respect in L.E.A.N. context means:

**1. Trusting People's Capability**
- Operators know the work best
- They can solve problems
- They want to do good work
- They have ideas worth hearing

**2. Developing People**
- Investing in training
- Teaching problem-solving
- Providing growth opportunities
- Building on strengths

**3. Listening and Acting**
- Seeking input genuinely
- Implementing suggestions
- Explaining decisions
- Valuing frontline knowledge

**4. Creating Good Work**
- Safe environment
- Ergonomic workstations
- Right tools and equipment
- Clear expectations
- Meaningful work

**5. Job Security Through Improvement**
- L.E.A.N. gains don't mean layoffs
- Freed capacity used for growth
- Cross-training and development
- Long-term thinking

### Respect in Practice

**Scenario: L.E.A.N. Improvement Eliminates 2 Hours of Work**

**Disrespectful Approach:**
```
Improvement frees 2 hours/day
    ↓
Lay off person or cut hours
    ↓
Message: "If you improve, you'll lose your job"
    ↓
Result: Resistance to all future improvements
```

**Respectful Approach:**
```
Improvement frees 2 hours/day
    ↓
Redeploy person to other value-add work
(Incoming work, new product, insource work,
 improvement projects, cross-training)
    ↓
Message: "Improvements help us grow and secure jobs"
    ↓
Result: Everyone motivated to improve
```

**Toyota's No-Layoff Philosophy:**

"We don't lay people off due to efficiency improvements. We find more work or bring work in-house. This is how we gain trust for continuous improvement."

### Respecting Frontline Knowledge

**The People Doing the Work Know:**
- Where the wastes are
- What frustrates them daily
- How to do it better
- What gets in the way
- What customers really care about

**Leader's Job:**
- Draw out this knowledge
- Provide framework for improvement
- Remove barriers
- Implement ideas
- Recognize contributions

**Example: Operator Insight**

```
Engineer designs fixture from prints, CAD.
Spends 2 weeks. Proud of design.

Operator looks at it:
"This part here will accumulate chips and cause jams."

Engineer's choice:
A) Defensive: "I designed it to print, it's fine."
B) Respectful: "Good catch! Let's modify it. What would work better?"

Option B leads to better fixture and engaged operator.
Option A leads to resentment and future silence.
```

### Humble Inquiry

**Concept from Edgar Schein:**

**Humble Inquiry:** Asking questions out of genuine curiosity, not to:
- Prove you're smart
- Trap someone
- Confirm what you already think
- Manipulate toward your answer

**Three Types of Humility:**

**1. Basic Humility** (status-based)
- Respecting higher status
- Example: New employee asking senior operator
- Natural in hierarchy

**2. Optional Humility** (earned through performance)
- Respecting demonstrated expertise
- Example: Asking skilled machinist for advice
- Choice based on competence

**3. Here-and-Now Humility** (situational)
- Recognizing dependence on others in this moment
- Example: Leader asking operator about process
- **This is what L.E.A.N. requires!**

**Here-and-Now Humility in L.E.A.N.:**

Leader realizes:
- "I need this person's knowledge to understand"
- "They are the expert in this context"
- "I am genuinely curious what they know"
- "My status doesn't mean I have all answers"

This creates:
- Open communication
- Knowledge sharing
- Problem-solving collaboration
- Mutual respect

## 15.4 Building Problem-Solving Capability

### From Firefighting to Problem Prevention

**Typical Organization:**
- Reactive: Problems solved as they occur
- Hero culture: Firefighters celebrated
- Same problems recur regularly
- No time for prevention
- Exhausting, ineffective

**L.E.A.N. Organization:**
- Proactive: Root causes addressed
- Prevention culture: Problem preventers celebrated
- Problems solved permanently
- Time invested in prevention
- Sustainable, effective

### The A3 Thinking Process

**What is A3?**
- Named for A3 paper size (11x17")
- Story of problem-solving on one page
- Left-to-right logical flow
- Visual, concise, data-based

**A3 Structure:**

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    A3 PROBLEM SOLVING                     │
├────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. BACKGROUND          │ 5. COUNTERMEASURES              │
│                        │                                 │
│ Why is this important? │ What will we do?                │
│ Business context       │ How will it address root cause? │
│                        │ Who, what, when?                │
├────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. CURRENT CONDITION   │ 6. IMPLEMENTATION PLAN          │
│                        │                                 │
│ What is happening?     │ Detailed action plan            │
│ Data, photos, metrics  │ Timeline, owners                │
│                        │ Resources needed                │
├────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. GOAL / TARGET       │ 7. FOLLOW-UP                    │
│                        │                                 │
│ What should happen?    │ How will we verify?             │
│ Specific, measurable   │ Check effectiveness             │
│ target                 │ Standardize if successful       │
├────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS                                   │
│                                                           │
│ Why is it happening? (5 Whys, Fishbone)                  │
│ Data to verify root cause                                │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**The A3 is NOT:**
- Just a form to fill out
- One person working alone
- Something done and filed away

**The A3 IS:**
- A structured thinking process
- Collaborative problem-solving
- A communication tool
- Coaching opportunity
- Living document (updated as learning occurs)

### Teaching Problem-Solving

**The Coaching Kata:**

**Not This:**
```
Problem occurs
   ↓
Leader solves it
   ↓
Problem fixed (for now)
   ↓
Team didn't learn anything
```

**But This:**
```
Problem occurs
   ↓
Leader coaches team through problem-solving
   ↓
Team develops solution with guidance
   ↓
Problem fixed AND team more capable
```

**Coaching Questions (Kata Pattern):**

1. **"What is the target condition?"**
   - Forces clarity on desired state
   - Specific, measurable

2. **"What is the actual condition now?"**
   - Go see, measure
   - Facts, not opinions

3. **"What obstacles are preventing us from reaching target?"**
   - Identify barriers
   - Which is most important?

4. **"What is the next step?"**
   - What will you try?
   - When will you try it?
   - What do you expect to happen?

5. **"When can we go see what we learned?"**
   - Sets follow-up
   - PDCA cycle
   - Accountability

**Example Coaching Dialogue:**

```
Operator: "We keep having rework on this part."

Leader: "What is the target condition for this part?"
Operator: "Zero defects, first-time through."

Leader: "And what is the actual condition now?"
Operator: "We're getting about 90% FTT. 10% need rework."

Leader: "What defect are you seeing?"
Operator: "Dimension out of spec on diameter."

Leader: "Okay, so 10% of parts have diameter out of spec.
         What obstacles might be causing this?"
Operator: "Could be tool wear, could be material variation,
          could be program issue..."

Leader: "Good. Which do you think is most likely?"
Operator: "Probably tool wear. I notice it getting worse
          through the run."

Leader: "How could we verify that?"
Operator: "We could measure dimension at beginning, middle,
          and end of batch. Plot it."

Leader: "Great idea. When can you do that?"
Operator: "Next run is tomorrow. I'll measure every 10 parts."

Leader: "Perfect. When can we look at the data together?"
Operator: "Tomorrow afternoon?"

Leader: "I'll come by at 2 PM. We'll see what you learned
        and figure out the next step."

(Next day, review data together, confirm root cause,
 implement countermeasure, follow up again)
```

### Developing Problem-Solving Culture

**Make Problems Visible:**
- Problem boards
- Andon systems
- Defect displays
- Metric boards showing gaps

**Respond to Problems Constructively:**
- No blame
- Focus on process, not person
- Curiosity: "What can we learn?"
- Appreciate people surfacing issues

**Provide Time and Resources:**
- Schedule improvement time
- Training in problem-solving tools
- Support from engineering, quality, etc.
- Budget for small improvements

**Recognize Problem-Solving Efforts:**
- Celebrate permanent solutions
- Share success stories
- Recognize attempts, not just wins
- Make improvement part of performance reviews

**Teach Tools:**
- 5 Whys
- Fishbone diagrams
- Pareto analysis
- PDCA cycle
- A3 thinking
- Root cause analysis

## 15.5 Coaching and Mentoring

### The Leader as Coach

**Traditional Manager Role:**
- Expert with all answers
- Makes decisions
- Directs activity
- Checks results

**L.E.A.N. Leader Role:**
- Developer of people
- Asks questions
- Coaches thinking
- Builds capability

**Why Coaching Matters:**

Short-term:
- Solving problems yourself is faster
- You get quick results

Long-term:
- Developing problem-solvers is powerful
- Organization capability grows
- You're not the bottleneck
- Sustainable improvement

**Toyota Saying:**
"We don't build cars. We build people who build cars."

### Coaching Fundamentals

**The Coaching Mindset:**

Belief that:
- People want to do well
- They can develop capability
- Your job is to develop them
- Their growth is your success

**Coaching vs. Telling:**

| Coaching (Develop Thinking) | Telling (Give Answer) |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------|
| "What do you think?" | "Here's what to do" |
| "What have you tried?" | "I've seen this before" |
| "What's the root cause?" | "The problem is..." |
| "What would happen if...?" | "That won't work" |
| "Let's go see together" | "I'll check reports" |

**When to Coach vs. When to Tell:**

**Coach When:**
- Person has (or could have) capability
- Time allows for development
- Learning is valuable
- Problem will recur
- Building long-term capability

**Tell When:**
- Safety emergency
- No time for development (rare)
- Person truly lacks knowledge
- One-time unique situation
- After coaching hasn't worked

**Rule of Thumb:** Coach 80% of the time, tell 20%.

### Mentoring for L.E.A.N.

**What is Mentoring?**

More than coaching on single problem:
- Long-term developmental relationship
- Mentor shares experience, wisdom
- Guides career growth
- Provides perspective
- Challenges and supports

**Formal Mentoring Programs:**

Pair experienced L.E.A.N. practitioners with:
- New supervisors
- Operators moving into leadership
- Engineers learning L.E.A.N.
- Anyone championing improvement

**Structure:**
- Regular meetings (weekly or biweekly)
- Specific development goals
- Real problems as learning vehicle
- Safe space for questions
- Mutual commitment

**Mentor Responsibilities:**
- Share experiences (successes and failures)
- Ask powerful questions
- Provide resources and connections
- Challenge thinking
- Offer encouragement
- Follow up and hold accountable

**Mentee Responsibilities:**
- Come with specific questions
- Try things between meetings
- Be open to feedback
- Reflect on learning
- Take ownership of development

**Example Mentoring Topics:**
- How to lead a gemba walk effectively
- Dealing with resistance to change
- Facilitating a kaizen event
- Root cause analysis for complex problem
- Building business case for improvement
- Coaching difficult conversations

### Learning by Doing

**70-20-10 Model:**

```
10% - Formal learning (classes, reading)
20% - Learning from others (mentoring, observation)
70% - Learning by doing (experience, projects)
```

**Implication for L.E.A.N.:**

Don't just send people to training and expect change.

**Better Approach:**
- 10%: L.E.A.N. fundamentals training
- 20%: Observe gemba walks, shadow mentor, join kaizen
- 70%: Lead improvement project with coaching support

**Deliberate Practice:**

Not just doing, but:
- Focused on specific skill
- Immediate feedback
- Reflection on performance
- Coaching to improve
- Repeat with refinement

**Example: Learning to Lead Kaizen**

1. **Observe** experienced facilitator (20%)
2. **Co-facilitate** with mentor (70% - doing with support)
3. **Reflect** on what worked, what didn't (feedback)
4. **Read** about facilitation techniques (10%)
5. **Lead** next kaizen with mentor observing (70% - doing)
6. **Debrief** with mentor (20% - learning from coach)
7. **Repeat** with increasing independence

## 15.6 Creating a Culture of Continuous Improvement

### What is "Culture"?

**Simple Definition:**
"The way we do things around here"

**More Specifically:**
- Shared beliefs and values
- Unwritten rules
- Accepted behaviors
- What gets rewarded
- What gets punished
- Stories people tell

**Culture Eats Strategy for Breakfast:**
- Best L.E.A.N. tools won't work in wrong culture
- Culture determines what actually happens
- Changing culture is hard but essential

### Elements of L.E.A.N. Culture

**1. Problem Awareness**
- Problems are opportunities
- Making problems visible is good
- Hiding problems is bad
- Everyone responsible for improvement

**2. Customer Focus**
- Everything starts with customer value
- Internal customers matter too
- Eliminate waste = better serve customer
- Fast response to customer needs

**3. Respect for People**
- Everyone's ideas valued
- Develop people's capability
- Job security through improvement
- Teamwork over individual heroics

**4. Continuous Improvement (Kaizen)**
- Never satisfied with status quo
- Small improvements daily
- Everyone involved
- Standardize, then improve standard

**5. Go See (Genchi Genbutsu)**
- Facts from gemba, not desk
- Understand root causes deeply
- Trust but verify
- Base decisions on data from source

**6. Long-Term Thinking**
- Short-term sacrifices for long-term gain
- Invest in people and capability
- Sustainable pace
- Build for future

### Cultural Indicators

**Signs of Good L.E.A.N. Culture:**
- Problems discussed openly in meetings
- Operators stop production for quality issues
- Improvement suggestions abundant
- Standardized work respected and updated
- Leaders spend most time at gemba
- Cross-functional collaboration smooth
- Mistakes treated as learning opportunities
- Customers complimented on responsiveness

**Signs of Poor L.E.A.N. Culture:**
- Problems hidden until crisis
- Pressure to run regardless of quality
- No suggestions or ideas shared
- Standards ignored or non-existent
- Leaders rarely leave office
- Departments blame each other
- Mistakes hidden or blamed
- Customers complain about delays

### Changing Culture

**Reality: Culture change is slow**
- Takes years, not months
- Requires persistent consistency
- Leadership must model
- No shortcuts

**Approach:**

**1. Start with Leadership Behavior**
- Can't delegate culture change
- Leaders must embody L.E.A.N.
- Actions speak louder than words
- Be the change you want

**2. Create Visible Systems**
- Daily management boards
- Standard work
- Problem response process
- Improvement tracking
- Recognition programs

**3. Train and Develop**
- L.E.A.N. fundamentals for all
- Problem-solving skills
- Leaders as teachers
- Continuous learning

**4. Align Rewards and Consequences**
- What gets rewarded?
- What gets punished?
- Do they align with L.E.A.N. values?
- Change if not

**5. Celebrate and Share Wins**
- Make successes visible
- Tell improvement stories
- Recognize contributors
- Build momentum

**6. Be Patient and Persistent**
- Expect setbacks
- Don't give up
- Consistent message
- Stay the course

### Culture Change Example

**Traditional Culture:**
"Don't bring me problems, bring me solutions"

**Message Sent:**
- Problems are bad
- Don't surface issues
- Leaders don't want to hear

**Result:**
- Problems hidden
- Issues fester
- Crises erupt

**L.E.A.N. Culture:**
"Bring me problems! Let's solve them together"

**Message Sent:**
- Problems are opportunities
- We value awareness
- We'll support you

**Result:**
- Problems surfaced early
- Collaborative solving
- Continuous improvement

**How to Change:**
1. Leaders announce new expectation
2. Leaders model - thank people for raising problems
3. Leaders go to gemba, ask about problems
4. Leaders coach problem-solving
5. Leaders recognize problem-solving efforts
6. Repeat consistently for months/years
7. Culture gradually shifts

## 15.7 Overcoming Resistance to Change

### Why People Resist L.E.A.N.

**Common Reasons:**

**1. Fear**
- "Will I lose my job if we get more efficient?"
- "Can I learn these new methods?"
- "What if I fail?"

**2. Comfort with Current State**
- "We've always done it this way"
- "It works fine now"
- "Why change what's not broken?"

**3. Lack of Understanding**
- "What's L.E.A.N.?"
- "Why are we doing this?"
- "This seems like just another program"

**4. Past Negative Experience**
- "We tried this before, it didn't work"
- "Last 'improvement' made things worse"
- "Management always changes direction"

**5. Loss of Control**
- "I'm good at current way"
- "New way makes me feel incompetent"
- "I lose my expertise"

**6. Workload Concerns**
- "More work on top of everything else"
- "No time for improvement"
- "Already overwhelmed"

**7. Skepticism**
- "Flavor of the month"
- "Management not committed"
- "Won't really change anything"

### The Change Curve

**Emotional Journey of Change:**

```
      High│
          │    1.            3.
          │   Denial    Exploration
          │     /\          /
Emotional │    /  \        /
Response  │   /    \      /      4.
          │  /      \    /    Commitment
          │ /     2. \  /       ___/
          │/     Resistance  ___/
     Low  │         \    ___/
          └──────────┴──────────────→ Time
```

**1. Denial**
- "This doesn't apply to us"
- "It will blow over"
- Ignoring change

**2. Resistance**
- Frustration, anger
- "This won't work"
- Active opposition
- **Most difficult phase**

**3. Exploration**
- Trying new ways
- Learning
- Some setbacks
- Beginning to see benefits

**4. Commitment**
- New way is normal
- Supporting change
- Helping others

**Leader's Job:**
Help people move through curve faster

### Strategies to Overcome Resistance

**1. Communicate the Why**

People need to understand:
- Why change is necessary
- What problem are we solving?
- What's in it for them?
- What's in it for company?
- What happens if we don't change?

**Example:**
```
Instead of: "We're implementing L.E.A.N."

Try: "Our lead times are 10 days while competitors
     are 5 days. We're losing business. L.E.A.N. will
     help us reduce lead time, serve customers better,
     and secure our jobs through growth."
```

**2. Involve People Early**

- Don't announce, engage
- Ask for input
- Include in planning
- Pilot in friendly area first
- Volunteers, not volun-told

**3. Address Fears Directly**

**Fear: Job Loss**
"We commit: No layoffs due to L.E.A.N. improvements.
Freed capacity goes to new work and growth."

**Fear: Can't Learn**
"We'll train you. We'll support you. We'll go slow.
You won't be alone."

**4. Start Small, Show Success**

- Pilot project
- Quick win
- Visible results
- Share success story
- Build credibility
- Expand from there

**5. Provide Training and Support**

- L.E.A.N. fundamentals training
- Tools training (5S, SMED, etc.)
- Problem-solving skills
- Coaching and mentoring
- Resources for improvement

**6. Recognize and Celebrate**

- Early adopters
- Improvement efforts
- Small wins
- Team successes
- Individual contributions
- Make it positive

**7. Address Resisters Appropriately**

**Types of Resisters:**

**Honest Skeptics:**
- Have legitimate concerns
- Approach: Listen, address concerns, involve them
- Often become strongest supporters once convinced

**Comfortable:**
- Just like current state
- Approach: Show benefits, make it easy, support them
- Usually come along eventually

**Active Resisters:**
- Undermine change
- Spread negativity
- Approach: Private conversation, understand why,
  set expectations, consequences if continues

**Script for Active Resisters:**

```
"I understand you have concerns about L.E.A.N. I'd like
to hear them. [Listen] I appreciate your honesty. Here's
what I need: You don't have to be the biggest cheerleader,
but I need you to give it a fair try and not undermine
the effort. If after trying it fairly you still have
concerns, we'll discuss. But I can't have you sabotaging
before we've even tried. Can you commit to that?"
```

**If they continue to resist:** May not be right fit.

### Change Management Framework

**Kotter's 8 Steps (Applied to L.E.A.N.):**

**1. Create Urgency**
- Share competitive data
- Customer feedback
- Current state problems
- Business case for change

**2. Build Guiding Coalition**
- Leadership team alignment
- Cross-functional support
- Informal influencers
- Change champions

**3. Form Strategic Vision**
- Clear picture of L.E.A.N. future
- How it differs from today
- Benefits to organization
- Compelling direction

**4. Enlist Volunteers**
- Communicate vision widely
- Inspire, don't mandate
- Make it attractive
- Opt-in where possible

**5. Enable Action**
- Remove barriers
- Provide training
- Allocate resources
- Empower teams

**6. Generate Short-Term Wins**
- 3-6 month victories
- Visible, meaningful
- Celebrate publicly
- Build momentum

**7. Sustain Acceleration**
- Don't declare victory early
- Keep pushing
- Tackle bigger problems
- Deepen culture change

**8. Institute Change**
- Embed in systems
- Update procedures
- Hire/promote for fit
- New normal

## Summary

L.E.A.N. leadership and culture are the foundation that makes tools and techniques effective. Key principles:

**Leadership:**
- Spend time at gemba, not office
- Ask questions, don't give all answers
- Develop people's problem-solving capability
- Model L.E.A.N. behaviors consistently
- Create environment where problems can surface

**Gemba Walks:**
- Regular, genuine engagement at workplace
- Observe process, ask questions, learn
- Coach and develop, don't audit and criticize
- Follow up on commitments made

**Respect for People:**
- Trust frontline knowledge
- Develop capability
- Provide good work and job security
- Humble inquiry and listening

**Problem-Solving Culture:**
- Problems are treasures
- Teach problem-solving thinking
- A3 and coaching kata
- PDCA cycle
- Learn from both success and failure

**Continuous Improvement Culture:**
- Never satisfied with status quo
- Everyone involved daily
- Small steps, sustained
- Long-term perspective
- Celebrate progress

**Change Management:**
- Expect resistance
- Communicate why
- Involve people
- Start small
- Be patient and persistent
- Recognize and celebrate

**Remember:** "Culture eats strategy for breakfast." The best L.E.A.N. tools will fail without the right culture. Leaders create culture through their daily actions.

## Practical Exercises

**Exercise 1: Gemba Walk Practice**
Conduct a 30-minute gemba walk in a work area:
- Observe without interrupting
- Ask open-ended questions
- Look for the 4 S's (Safety, Standard Work, Status, Suggestions)
- Identify 3 wastes
- Note 2 follow-up items
- Write reflection: What did you learn?

**Exercise 2: Coaching Practice**
Role-play coaching scenario:
- One person presents a problem
- Other person coaches using these questions only:
  - "What is the target condition?"
  - "What is actual condition?"
  - "What obstacles are preventing you?"
  - "What is your next step?"
  - "When can we check results?"
- Debrief: How did it feel? What was hard? What worked?

**Exercise 3: A3 Development**
Select a real problem in your area:
- Complete an A3 using the 7-step format
- Use actual data, photos, evidence
- Apply 5 Whys for root cause
- Develop countermeasures
- Present to a colleague for feedback

**Exercise 4: Culture Assessment**
Assess your organization's culture:
- Use "Signs of Good/Poor L.E.A.N. Culture" list
- Score each indicator (1-5 scale)
- Identify strengths and gaps
- Prioritize 3 areas for improvement
- Create action plan for each

**Exercise 5: Change Resistance Plan**
For an upcoming change:
- List potential sources of resistance
- Identify which type of resistance each represents
- Plan specific strategies to address each
- Create communication plan
- Determine how you'll measure acceptance
